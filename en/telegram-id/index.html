A Telegram ID is a unique number that Telegram assigns to every account, bot, group and channel. You don't choose it and you can't edit it. It is different from your username, which is a public name you pick, and from your phone number, which you sign in with. Bots and "Log in with Telegram" sites receive your ID so they can tell you apart from everyone else.

What a Telegram ID is

Inside Telegram, every user, bot, group and channel is a "peer", and Telegram's API documentation says each peer has an ID: a unique 64-bit number used to identify that specific user, chat or channel. Names change, usernames come and go, but apps refer to you by this number.

The Bot API describes the same field for a user or bot as a unique identifier, and notes that it may have more than 32 significant bits but at most 52. In plain terms, it is a whole number, and it can be a long one. Telegram's own configuration shows what one looks like: it lists the official Telegram account that posts feature news as 777000, and the anti-spam account as 5434988373.

One detail surprises people. The documentation says the ID sequences of users, chats and channels overlap, so the same number can belong to one user and to an unrelated channel. Apps tell them apart by type, which is why bots write group and channel IDs differently.

Telegram ID numbers for groups and channels

Bots see chats with signs and prefixes added, and Telegram's documentation gives the exact rule:

  • Users and bots: a positive number, the same in the Bot API as everywhere else.
  • Basic groups: the same number made negative.
  • Supergroups and channels: 1000000000000 is added to the number and the result is made negative, so it starts with -100.

That is why a channel ID in a bot's settings looks like -100 followed by more digits, while your own ID is positive. If you're setting up a group bot and it asks for "the chat ID", the leading minus sign is part of it.

Why apps and bots ask for your Telegram ID

Most of the time nobody has to ask. When you message a bot, it receives your ID together with your public name, username and profile pictures. Telegram's FAQ lists what bots can see, and its privacy policy says bot developers can get your public account data. The ID is how a bot remembers you between chats: your language, your settings, what you ordered.

Sites that offer "Log in with Telegram" work the same way. Telegram's login documentation says the basic profile scope gives the site your user ID, name, username and profile photo. Your phone number goes to the site only if it asks for that separately and you agree.

You may be asked for an ID directly in a few situations:

  1. A group or channel admin sets up a bot and needs to tell it which chat or which admins to trust.
  2. A service you signed up for wants to link your Telegram account to its own account.
  3. A developer is testing their own bot.

If a stranger asks for your ID together with a login code or password, stop. Telegram's FAQ says never to share passwords or Telegram codes with a bot, and that applies to people as well.

How to see your own Telegram ID

Telegram's pages don't describe a profile field in the official apps that shows your numeric ID, so we won't invent a menu path. What the documentation does make clear is that any bot you message receives the ID. A bot you trust, such as one your own group uses, can show it back to you.

There are many third-party "ID bots" and lookup sites. We don't recommend any of them. Telegram's FAQ advises treating any bot as a stranger, and its privacy policy says bots are completely independent from Telegram. If you need the number, use a bot you already rely on, or ask the admin who needs it how their own bot reads it.

Privacy: what someone can do with your Telegram ID

On its own, very little. Telegram's API documentation is direct about it: if an app has only a user, channel or supergroup ID without any kind of access hash, it cannot interact with that peer. A number alone doesn't open a chat with you, reveal your phone number or show your profile to someone who has never seen you.

Telegram also documents tg://user?id= links, but its link documentation calls them an abstraction offered by the Bot API that normal clients should ignore. So a link built from your ID is not a reliable way for strangers to reach you either.

What decides who can find you is your username and your privacy settings. If you want fewer people to reach you, remove your username and keep your phone number limited to contacts. For what else your profile shows, such as gifts, see how to hide Telegram gifts.

Telegram ID lookup: what it can and can't tell you

"Telegram ID lookup" services promise to turn a number into a profile or the other way round. Telegram's documentation explains why they are limited: an ID without an access hash can't be used to interact with a user. Telegram's privacy policy says a username, screen name and profile pictures are public, and that public data is what any outside service can work with.

The safer habit is to look people up by their public username in Telegram's own search, and to treat any site that asks you to sign in with your phone number to "see who looked you up" as a red flag. Questions people ask

What is my Telegram ID number?

It is a number Telegram assigned to your account when you signed up. It never appears as part of your profile name or username. Any bot you message receives it, because that is how the Bot API identifies users.

Is a Telegram ID the same as a username?

No. A username is a public @name you choose, can change or remove. The ID is a number Telegram assigns, and you can't pick or edit it.

Can someone find me with my Telegram ID?

Not directly. Telegram's API documentation says that with only a user ID and no access hash, an app cannot interact with that user. Your public username and phone number settings decide who can find you.

Why does a bot ask for my Telegram ID?

Usually it doesn't need to ask: when you message a bot, it already receives your ID along with your public name and username. Bots use the ID to remember you, for example to save your settings.

Should I use a Telegram ID lookup site?

We don't recommend any. Telegram's FAQ advises treating any bot as a stranger, and a lookup service is a third party you would hand information to.
