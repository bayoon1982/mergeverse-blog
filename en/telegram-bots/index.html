Telegram bots are small programs that run inside Telegram and talk to you through a chat. They are special accounts with no phone number behind them, and they can answer commands, search for things, open full apps inside Telegram and take payments in Telegram Stars. A bot can't write to you first: you start it, and you can stop or block it at any time.

What a Telegram bot is

Telegram's own definition is short: bots are small applications that run entirely within the Telegram app. Someone writes a program, connects it to Telegram through the Bot API, and the program answers messages through a bot account.

From your side, a bot looks like a chat, with a few differences that Telegram's bot documentation spells out:

  • It needs no phone number to set up.
  • It has no "last seen" or online status. You see a "bot" label instead.
  • Its username normally ends in "bot", like @examplebot. A few older official bots such as @gif and @wiki skip the suffix.
  • It can't start a conversation. You have to message it or add it to a group first.

The last point matters for safety. If a "bot" seems to have contacted you out of nowhere, either you started it earlier or it is not a bot at all.

What bots can do for you

Telegram's introduction lists the main uses. Bots can stand in for a website as a mini app, connect AI chatbots, help manage business accounts, take payments, host games, work with other services and devices, and act as small custom tools.

In practice you will meet bots in a few shapes:

Shape How you use it
Chat bot You send messages or commands, it replies in the chat
Inline bot You type its @username in any chat, pick a result, and it is sent there
Mini app A full interface that opens inside Telegram
Group bot An admin adds it to a group, where it moderates or answers
Business bot A Telegram Business user connects it to their own account to reply in their chats

Telegram also runs some services through bots of its own. Its client configuration, for example, names @PremiumBot as the official Telegram Premium bot, and stickers are managed through the @Stickers mini app (see Telegram stickers).

How to find and start a Telegram bot

You can reach a bot in two ways: by searching its username, or by opening its link. Every bot has a link of the form t.me/botusername, and Telegram's documentation says it opens a conversation with the bot. For how Telegram search works in general, see our guide to Telegram search.

To start one:

  1. Search for the bot's exact username, or open its t.me link.
  2. Check the username letter by letter. Copies of popular bots often use a name one character off.
  3. Open the chat and press Start at the bottom of the screen.
  4. Read the bot's first message. Telegram asks developers to support /start, /help and /settings, so /help usually shows what the bot does.

Some links carry extra data, like t.me/examplebot?start=something. That just tells the bot where you came from; it is passed only when you start the bot in a private chat.

Inline bots: using a bot in any chat

Inline mode lets you use a bot without leaving the chat you're in. Type the bot's @username in the message field of any chat, add a keyword, and the bot shows results above the keyboard. Tap one and it is sent to that chat as a normal message.

The person you're talking to doesn't need to have the bot. Telegram's apps use inline bots themselves: the client configuration names the inline bot the apps use to search for music. Only bots whose developer turned inline mode on in @BotFather can work this way.

Mini apps: full apps inside Telegram

A mini app is a bot with a real interface: buttons, pages and forms, opened inside Telegram instead of a browser. Telegram's documentation lists the ways one can open:

  • a menu button next to the message field in the bot's chat;
  • a button under a bot's message, or on the bot's keyboard;
  • a Launch app button on the bot's profile, if the bot has a main mini app;
  • a direct link like t.me/botusername/appname, from any chat;
  • the attachment menu, if the bot asked to be added there;
  • inline mode, in any chat.

Mini apps follow their own Terms of Service for Mini Apps, which Telegram's privacy policy points to. Treat a mini app like any website: don't type passwords or Telegram login codes into one.

Paying a bot with Telegram Stars

When a bot sells something digital, such as a service or a piece of content, it has to charge in Telegram Stars. The bot payments documentation says all such transactions must use Stars, with the currency code XTR. Stars are Telegram's in-app currency; we explain them in what Telegram Stars are.

Here is how a payment goes:

  1. The bot sends an invoice: a message with a photo, a description of what you're buying and a Pay button.
  2. You tap Pay, and Telegram opens its own payment screen.
  3. You confirm the amount in Stars.

If you don't have enough Stars, Telegram's documentation says you can get them from Telegram through Apple or Google in-app purchases, or through @PremiumBot. Buy them only from Telegram itself.

If something goes wrong, the bot is responsible, not Telegram. Developers must answer a /paysupport command and handle disputes themselves, and they can issue refunds in Stars. Bots are also asked to show their terms, usually through a /terms command. Stars are used for gifts too; see what Telegram gifts are.

How to stop or block a bot

Telegram's FAQ says you can block a bot the same way you block a human user. Open the bot's chat or profile and choose Block. Some apps also show a Stop Bot button in the bot's profile.

Before you block, check /help: many bots have their own command to mute notifications or unsubscribe.

Privacy and verified bots

What a bot learns about you is limited. The FAQ says a bot sees your public name, username and profile pictures, plus the messages you send it. It does not see your phone number or last seen status unless you share them. In groups, a bot by default sees only messages meant for it, unless its developer turned off privacy mode. Telegram's privacy policy adds that bots are completely independent from Telegram, and that opening a link a bot gives you can reveal your IP address to that website.

The FAQ's advice is to treat a bot like a stranger. Never send it passwords, Telegram login codes or bank details.

As for checkmarks, Telegram's verification page says only Telegram can assign the official verification checkmark. It appears on the profile, in your chat list and in search, and it means Telegram approved that bot's application. Approved third-party services can add their own icons, which look different and sit before the name. Open the profile to see who verified the bot and why. Your own account details work the same way with any app that asks for them; read what a Telegram ID is and Telegram username for what you share.

Questions people ask

Can a Telegram bot see my phone number?

No, unless you share it. Telegram's FAQ says bots see your public name, username, profile pictures and the messages you send them, but not your phone number or last seen status.

How do I stop a Telegram bot?

Block it the same way you block a person. Some apps also show a Stop Bot button in the bot's profile, and many bots list a command to silence them under /help.

Can a bot message me first?

No. Telegram's bot documentation says bots can't start conversations with users. You have to message it or add it to a group first.

How do I pay a bot on Telegram?

For digital goods and services, bots send an invoice with a Pay button, and payment is in Telegram Stars. You buy Stars from Telegram through Apple or Google in-app purchases or through @PremiumBot.

How do I know a bot is real?

Check the username carefully and look for Telegram's official checkmark, which only Telegram can assign. A different icon before the name means a third-party verifier; open the profile to see who verified it.
